Week 3 Recap — Market Sizing and TCO
Name: Ruiyuan Yin

This week, I built TAM, SAM, and SOM metrics for the US market for Fari, Senpai, and Sentinel Prime AI. Fari used CMS nursing facility data, Senpai used NCES public school data, and Sentinel used BLS security personnel employment data. The results showed that Fari's SAM was 7,395 larger institutions, Senpai's SAM was 28,218 target schools, and Sentinel's three-year base SOM was 276 deployment opportunities. When calculating Sentinel's market size, I separated the direct deployment industry from the contract security industry. Contract security companies may buy and operate systems for clients, but the number of security personnel cannot be directly used as the number of client locations. This approach makes the market scope clearer.

I also built a five-year TCO model for Aido Rover to compare the cost of Rover versus human patrols. The calculations included costs for equipment, maintenance, batteries, electricity, network connectivity, labor, and vehicles. Both scenarios were compared based on a daily patrol distance of 20 kilometers. In the baseline scenario, Rover's five-year TCO is approximately $346,632, while manual patrols cost approximately $450,137, resulting in savings of approximately $103,505 over five years. Rover's average cost is $9.50 per kilometer, while manual patrols cost $12.33 per kilometer. Cumulative savings turn positive in year 4, with an estimated payback period of 38.0 months.

I also briefly compared the impact of different assumptions on the results. Daily patrol hours and wage levels have the greatest impact on the payback period, while electricity consumption has a minimal impact. Increasing daily electricity consumption from 3 kWh to 5 kWh yielded essentially no change in the results. In contrast, the more manual hours required and the higher the wage level, the more pronounced Rover's cost advantage becomes. Sentinel Prime AI, operating on a 24-hour daily basis, anticipates a payback period of approximately 7.6 months, largely influenced by human hours and wage levels.

Currently, some data in the model is still based on assumptions. The next crucial step is to publicly disclose the personnel allocation, patrol distances, shift schedules, and contract amounts in security contracts. Actual pilot tests could also record patrol kilometers, charging frequency, downtime, network costs, and human support time. This data will help subsequent calculations more closely approximate real-world operational conditions.
